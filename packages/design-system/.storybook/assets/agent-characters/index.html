Brand guide

(a.k.a. the house rules)

For brand and marketing: who they are, how to use them, the files and how a new one is made. A new agent goes through design.

Characters, not people.

Wally and nine agents from one kit, on duty around the clock. Each one is a job you can see. None of them pretends to be someone.

A crew with a host

Wally is the host and the only one in coral. You talk to Wally. The others work in the background and step forward when there’s news.

One face

Every agent wears Wally’s visor and light eyes. That’s why they read as one family at any size.

The job first

The job picks the name, the shape and the prop. Nothing on them is decoration.

Each one has a story.

Always in the same order: the job, then the name, then the shape and the prop.

What they never are.

They work in a security product, for the people who own the risk. Six rules keep them trustworthy.

Never human

No human faces, first names or gender. Anyone can tell an agent from a colleague.

Never childish

Calm eyes, no grins, no jokes. Cute, and good at the job.

Never chatty

No mouth and no small talk. Copy stays plain: “Sift marked 8 false positives”, never “Sift is on it!”

Never on bad news

A face never softens a warning. Errors and risks stay in plain interface text. Failed looks sober, never red.

Never noisy

Only the one with news moves. At 32 px and below they hold still.

Never off-model

One kit, one look. No redraws, no other styles, no generated look-alikes.

Use them as they are.

Pick a character, a size and a state. Change nothing else.

Sizes

Real pixels on one floor. Below 48 px it’s always the flat glyph, and it never moves.

Color

Each agent keeps its color everywhere. Coral is Wally’s alone. Red, amber and green belong to the interface: a body never signals status.

Take them with you.

The glyph is a clean SVG for 16–32 px. The render is a transparent PNG for anything bigger. Both come straight from the kit on this page.

From job to agent in a day.

Everything comes from the kit. Nothing is drawn from scratch.

  1. The jobOne line on what it does. That line is its role, and the role never changes.
  2. A nameOne word for the job: one or two syllables, up to six letters, no human names. Try it in a sentence: “Replay confirmed 2 of 5 attacks.”
  3. A headA shape from the library that echoes the job. Two agents share a shape only when their jobs are a pair, like Replay and Patch.
  4. A propOne detail that shows the job: a tool on the head, or the shape itself. A tool moves when the agent works.
  5. A colorFrom the palette, unlike its neighbors, never coral.
  6. The 20 px testNext to the cast at 20 px, it reads without its name.
  7. Design signs offBefore it ships anywhere. Make a draft in the builder above and bring it to design.

Design signs off.

Use what’s here freely. Anything new goes through design first, so the family stays one family.

  • A new agent, or a second one in a role.
  • A new shape, prop, color, pose or expression.
  • New motion, 3D or video.
